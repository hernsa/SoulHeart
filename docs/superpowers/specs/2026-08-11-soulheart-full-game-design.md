# SoulHeart — Full Game Story & World Design

**Date:** 2026-08-11
**Status:** Drafting (brainstorming)
**Purpose:** Expand SoulHeart from a 2-room vertical slice into a complete 6-area RPG with story, original mobs, bosses, and three endings. Third parallel world alongside Undertale and Deltarune.

---

## 1. Premise

**You fall through a crack that wasn't there before.**

SoulHeart is a **third parallel world** alongside Undertale and Deltarune — accessed through hairline cracks in the floor of an ordinary room, when nobody is looking. It does not appear on any floorplan. Nobody you ask later will remember seeing it.

You are a child. You fell. You don't remember climbing up to where you fell from. You wake up on cold grass in a place that feels like someone's memory of a place: the trees are right but the branches bend the wrong way, the houses are warm but the doors face inward, and every so often something says your name in a voice you almost know.

**This world has been edited before.** You can feel it. The rain here never lands. The save point remembers a save you didn't make. The shopkeeper hands you a receipt with prices you can't read.

The entity that did this calls itself the **Canon**. It believes there should only ever be **one** version of every story. It is currently editing this one down.

---

## 2. Story Thesis

**Canon vs. Index.**

The Canon is not a person. It is a *process* — the force that turns "things that happened once" into "things that happened always." It has been doing this to every story since stories existed. It works slowly. It only stops when the story is *finished* — when there is only one version left and nobody remembers there were others.

It came to this world because the cracks let in too many versions of too many things. A child who remembers two different childhoods. A tree that grows two different ways. A save file that writes itself.

The Canon is trying to **make this world resolve into one version of itself** — and in doing so, it is erasing the things that made the world *worth* being a world.

The player must choose whether to **fight the Canon** (Hollow ending: erase the cracks, the world becomes "perfect" and quiet), **accept it** (Keeper ending: the Canon finishes, the world is one thing forever, the player is part of it now), or **negotiate with it** (Wanderer ending: one crack closes, the rest stay open, the world remains plural and unfinished).

### Why a third parallel world (not a bridge)

Per Toby Fox's own framing, Undertale and Deltarune are **parallel worlds**, not a branching timeline. A fan-game that tries to be the bridge between them contradicts canon. Declaring SoulHeart a third parallel world with its own rules:

- **Cannot contradict** UT or DR canon (no human SOULs walking around — they are absorbed by Asgore during UT).
- **Can reference** UT/DR canon in the "echoes" sense: a skeleton who tips his cap and walks through a door that closes behind him; a flower that winks; a wisp that hums a tune the player doesn't remember learning.
- **Can use** familiar enemies as *echoes* — not the originals. "Froggit" in SoulHeart is the *echo* of a frog that existed in another world, drift-glued into this one. It talks the same way, for the same reasons. It is not the same frog.

## 3. Characters

### 3.1 Protagonist

**Soulheart** — silent protagonist. Striped sweater (cream-and-amber stripes — warm, not the cool blue of UT). Carries a single red SOUL (a heart). Their defining trait is that **they are still deciding who they are**. Canon wants to decide for them. The player decides for them. Other characters decide for them. The whole game is about whose version wins.

### 3.2 Guide

**Wisp** — small pale-amber ball of light. Hummed-into-being by the player character once, a long time ago in a world they can't quite remember. Wisp insists this is true and cannot be convinced otherwise. Wisp hums a 4-note leitmotif that recurs across the soundtrack and resolves differently in each area. Wisp is the player character's moral compass — but Wisp's compass points in two directions at once and Wisp refuses to choose.

*Voice note:* cheerful, kind, occasionally says something that sounds casual but is actually a revelation.

### 3.3 Antagonist

**The Canon** — appears as a tall figure in a long coat, face obscured by an index card. The card always shows the *current* canonical version of whatever it is looking at. Touching the Canon erases a thing. Talking to the Canon locks a thing. The Canon never speaks, but its footsteps sound like a book being closed.

*The Canon is not evil.* It is doing what Canon does. It has been doing this to better stories than yours. It does not understand why anyone would want to be more than one thing.

## 4. World — The Six Areas

### 4.1 The Hush

**Theme:** *Remembering safety.* You just fell. The world is being kind to you because it hasn't decided what you are yet.
**Look:** Greens and greys, rain that never lands (already exists). Trees bent slightly inward, as if listening.
**Mechanic introduced:** **SAVE** — the first save point the player touches, and the first time the game explicitly says "this remembers you." The save point's text is always slightly wrong: "Game saved." then "Game saved?" then "Game saved." again.
**Mobs:** Reminisc, Hushroom, Froggit, Whimsun.
**Exit:** Through a hollow tree that wasn't there before.

### 4.2 The Murmur

**Theme:** *Things talking in their sleep.* The world is busy. The player has to figure out which sounds are meant for them.
**Look:** Cooler palette. Pines that lean. Trees that hum when the player walks past.
**Mechanic introduced:** **Hush Path** — a puzzle: the trees only let you pass when you walk a specific silent path. The path is encoded in the sound it does *not* make.
**Mobs:** Pane-ic, Squi-sh, Loox.
**Exit:** Through a door the player didn't see before, behind a curtain of trees.

### 4.3 The Echo

**Theme:** *Your past selves, briefly.* The world remembers versions of you that didn't happen. They say things you almost said.
**Look:** Wet rocks, dim blues. Sound carries further than it should. Every surface shows the player's reflection one frame late.
**Mechanic introduced:** **Echo dialogue** — some NPCs say the player's last line back at them. The first time, it's charming. By the third, it's unsettling. By the fifth, it's a question.
**Mobs:** Senti-mint, Repeato, Moldsmal, Migosp.
**Exit:** Through a cave mouth that opens only when the player hums the right note.

### 4.4 Hometown Stand-In

**Theme:** *A place that looks familiar but the names are wrong.* The closest thing to "normal" the world offers. The closest thing to a town.
**Look:** Suburban blocks, warm light through windows. Street signs read correctly, mostly.
**Mechanic introduced:** **Shop + cameo** — The Reader runs a shop. Bones appears once. The town has a name on its welcome sign, but the name is written in pencil and rubs off when the player touches it.
**Mobs:** Toadally, Vegetoid, Pun-kin.
**Exit:** Through the town square. A child the player almost recognizes waves goodbye.

### 4.5 The Canon

**Theme:** *Editing.* The world starts deciding what it is. NPCs repeat themselves. Doors that used to lead somewhere now lead somewhere else.
**Look:** Industrial warmtones — gears, cogs, paper. Lightbulbs that flicker in time with the player's heartbeat.
**Mechanic introduced:** **Edit events** — small moments where the world re-writes itself. A bookshelf adds a book. A wall gains a window. The player can either accept or refuse.
**Mobs:** Nullaby, Quibble, Mar-gin.
**Mini-boss:** The Mourning Knight.
**Exit:** A hallway that wasn't there before. At the end, the door.

### 4.6 The Cracks

**Theme:** *The threshold.* The player reaches the source of the cracks. The Canon is here. The Index is here. The Old Dreamer is here. The ending choice is here.
**Look:** Mostly void. Light leaks through the cracks in the floor. Everything the player has collected hums in their inventory.
**Mechanic introduced:** **Ending branch** — three doors. The player chooses one. The walk to each door is short. The walk back is impossible.
**Mobs:** Loo-key, Re-mem-bran.
**Final boss:** The Index (three routes) / The Canon true form (Keeper only).
**Final NPC:** The Old Dreamer.
**Exit:** The way out is the way the player came in — through a crack that, after the ending, is no longer there.

## 5. Gameplay Systems (spec updates)

### 5.1 Existing systems (kept)

- Top-down movement, save points, FIGHT/ACT/ITEM/MERCY menus, fight bar, bullet hell dodge, typewriter dialogue, kill/spare counters, JSON save, audio system.
- Two existing rooms (Drizzle Fields, Grumble Woods) become **The Hush** (Areas 1) and **The Murmur** (Area 2) — renamed in dialogue but the rooms themselves stay.

### 5.2 New systems

**Wisp:** companion that follows the player through every area, lighting up dark rooms, opening a menu to hum the area's leitmotif fragment (UI hint: "Press Z near Wisp to listen"). Wisp is never in danger. Wisp is never optional.

**Save point echoes:** every save point has a "memory" that drifts — a line the player never said, attributed to a name they've never met. The first save point whispers the ending the player will pick before they know there is a choice.

**Edit events:** scripted moments in The Canon and The Cracks where the world re-writes. The player gets a "Flee / Refuse / Accept" prompt. Each choice changes a flag that ripples into one of the three endings.

**ACT menu expansion:** original mobs get unique ACT entries that don't just shift mood — they reveal *who the mob is*. ACT menu text is the heart of the game.

**Endings:**
- **Keeper (Hollow trap avoided + Canon faced):** The Canon is confronted. The player chooses whether the Canon finishes. If yes, the world becomes one thing. If no, the Canon fights. If defeated, the cracks stay open. The player's soul stays split. The credits roll over the world, which is now plural again.
- **Wanderer (mixed play):** One crack closes. The rest stay open. The world is half-remembered. The save points remember the player but not why. Wisp hums alone.
- **Hollow (every edit accepted, every mob killed):** The Canon finishes. The world is "perfect" and quiet. The player wakes up in their own bed. They don't remember falling. They don't remember playing. The save file is empty.

## 6. Tech Stack & Architecture (extension)

Godot 4.4.1, GDScript, GL Compatibility renderer, headless test runner. Additions:

- **Scene-per-area:** `scenes/rooms/{Hush,Murmur,Echo,Hometown,Canon,Cracks}.tscn`. Existing DrizzleFields → Hush, GrumbleWoods → Murmur.
- **Mob library:** `scripts/battle/enemy_library.gd` grows from 6 to 19 entries (13 original + 6 echoes). Same ACT-menu shape.
- **Mob art pipeline:** `assets/sprites/enemies/{original}_idle.png` + `_hurt.png` silhouettes, all 16×16 to 64×64, cutout-keyed black backgrounds (chroma cutout exists from B2 — reuse).
- **Tile sets per area:** `scripts/tiles/tiles.gd` adds `HUSH_STYLE`, `MURMUR_STYLE`, `ECHO_STYLE`, `HOMETOWN_STYLE`, `CANON_STYLE`, `CRACKS_STYLE` (6 styles).
- **Music:** 6 area themes (existing mus_drizzle/mus_snowdin become Hush/Murmur; new mus_echo/mus_hometown/mus_canon/mus_cracks). One Wisp leitmotif that recurs across all of them. Credits theme. Hollow ending theme (silence + one note).
- **SFX:** +12 new SFX (door-in-canon-area, edit-event-bell, echo-repeats, mob-specific ACT triggers, ending stings).
- **Wisp companion:** `scripts/companion/wisp.gd` + `scenes/Wisp.tscn`. Follows player, idle animation, hum on confirm.
- **Edit events:** `scripts/world/edit_event.gd` — generic prompt + flag-setter.
- **Save point echo:** `scripts/rooms/save_point.gd` extends existing with `_echo_text()` lookup.

## 7. Implementation — Plan Decomposition

This is too large for one plan. Decomposed into **4 sub-plans**, each self-contained, each shippable, each leaving the game playable:

**Plan A — Mob Roster Expansion + Wisp companion**
- +13 original mobs in enemy_library with ACT menus, spare conditions, attack patterns.
- Wisp companion follows player through all existing areas.
- Mob sprites (silhouettes + chroma cutout reuse).
- ~20 new dialogue files.
- **Shippable:** new mobs work everywhere, Wisp follows the player, existing 2 areas fully populated with the new roster.
- **Plan A: COMPLETE** (branch plan-a-mobs-wisp, 12 commits cd85f9d..3032531; 13 mobs, Wisp in both rooms, hum input, 4 wisp dialogue files, 3 mob intros).

**Plan B — Areas 3-6 (rooms + tiles + scenes)**
- New rooms: Echo, Hometown, Canon, Cracks.
- 4 new tile styles.
- Doors + transitions from Murmur → Echo → Hometown → Canon → Cracks.
- 2 area themes (Echo, Hometown). 4 new mob placements per area.
- **Shippable:** walkable Ruins → Snowdin → Echo → Hometown → Canon → Cracks. No bosses yet.
- **Plan B: COMPLETE** (branch plan-b-areas-3-6; Echo/Hometown/Canon/Cracks rooms, 4 tile styles, 2 area themes, 6-room door chain).

**Plan C — Bosses + Mini-boss + Final fight**
- The Mourning Knight (mini-boss).
- The Index (final boss, 3 forms).
- The Canon true form (Keeper-only).
- Boss ACT menus, multi-phase fight bar, save-before-boss flow.
- **Shippable:** walk the whole game, beat bosses, but ending choice has no consequences yet.

**Plan D — Story system + Endings + Music + Edit events + Delivery**
- Edit event system + 6 scripted edit events.
- Save point echoes.
- Ending choice at The Cracks.
- 3 endings (Keeper, Wanderer, Hollow).
- Wisp leitmotif + 4 new area themes + credits + Hollow silence-track.
- Final commit, export, delivery kit, README rewrite.
- **Shippable:** full playable game.

Each plan produces a working, testable build that adds content to the existing world.

## 8. Risks & Constraints

- **Scope:** 6 areas + 19 mobs + 3 endings + music + Wisp = ~6-8 weeks of focused work split across 4 plans. Smaller decomposition gives reviewable checkpoints.
- **IP safety:** all original mobs follow pun-name grammar but use new words; all cameos are silhouettes + jokes, no names; no UT/DR SOULs/characters walking around; explicit "third parallel world" framing; companion (Wisp) deliberately distinct from Flowey in silhouette, dialogue, motivation.
- **Asset sourcing:** all original mob sprites generated to fit existing chroma-cutout pipeline (Plan A reuses Plan T2 work). Music CC0/permissive from OpenGameArt/Labyrinth Open Source UT-style packs OR hand-composed 4-8 note area motifs. No ripped assets.
- **Canon safety:** spec explicitly references the existing 2-room "look" work (DrizzleFields/Hush, GrumbleWoods/Murmur rename only), so prior work remains valid.
- **Existing tests:** 36 existing test files — Plan A must pass all; Plan B/C/D adds tests per new system. Suite gate ("ALL TESTS PASSED" + permitted stderr only) holds for every plan.
